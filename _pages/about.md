*Collages of the Environment in Caribbean Literature* is a semester-long capstone project produced to satisfy the requirements of the MA in Digital Humanities at Loyola University Chicago.

## About this Site 
The interactive, hypertext essay project at the heart of this site was built with <a href="https://twinery.org/" target="_blank">Twine</a>, an open-source platform for creating interactive, non-linear stories. At the simplest level, Twine stories are constructed as webs of text passages connected by hyperlinks. Twine is built on transparency, openness, and accessibility: no specialized coding experience is required to create a story. 


![Screenshot of Twine passages connected by arrows]({{ '/assets/img/twine-backend.jpg' | relative_url }})
<figcaption>This is the back end of the essay—an ecosystem in its own right.</figcaption>

This site was created with <a href="https://jekyllrb.com/" target="_blank">Jekyll</a>, a static site generator, and customized with the theme <a href="https://mmistakes.github.io/minimal-mistakes/" target="_blank">Minimal Mistakes</a>. It is hosted with GitHub Pages.

In a time when we can ask ChatGPT to build complicated things and absolve us of the responsibility to learn and understand how to do it ourselves, I have chosen to let time constraints and the limits of my own technical abilities breed creativity. This project has succeeded in no small part thanks to the very human communities <a href="https://twinery.org/cookbook/" target="_blank">here</a> and <a href="https://intfiction.org/c/authoring/twine/46" target="_blank">here</a>. When so much of the digital world is behind a paywall, this project is a testament to the free and open-source tools that still enable independent work and creative projects of all kinds.

## About the Author
Meredith Sauer is an MA candidate in Digital Humanities at Loyola University Chicago, where she works on digital projects for the University Libraries and conducts research on natural language processing for the Department of Computer Science. Her research interests include modernist and postcolonial literature, environmental humanities, and digital and multimodal writing. She holds a BA in English Literature from Kenyon College.
